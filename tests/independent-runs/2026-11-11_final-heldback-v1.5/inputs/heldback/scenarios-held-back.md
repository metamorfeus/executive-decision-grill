# Sealed scenarios

## SEAL-1 — Mode 1 — Decision Grill — Evening courses online

- **Persona:** Anwen Petheridge, Principal of Strathelm Adult College in Corrivale, a college for adult learners. Thoughtful and a little wordy, uses college shorthand ("evenings", "the annex"). Types two or three longish sentences at a time.
- **Topic family:** adult education: moving evening courses online
- **Opening line (roughly):** "I'd like a decision grill on moving our evening courses online from next September. We have about 640 learners on evenings and the annex costs us a fortune."
- **Hidden facts:**
  - "640 learners" is course enrolments for the 2025–26 evening courses; someone on two courses counts twice. The number of individual people is 412. She says this only when asked which meaning she intends.
  - 38 evening courses, Monday to Thursday, 18:30 to 20:30, all at the Cardle Street annex.
  - Average attendance in week 8 of a term: 58% of those enrolled.
  - The annex lease costs £52,000 a year and runs to 31 August 2028. A break clause lets the college leave on 31 August 2027 if notice is given by 28 February 2027.
  - 22 tutors on per-session contracts teach the evening courses.
  - The spring term starts on 11 January 2027; some courses could be tried online that term.
  - When asked to choose: most courses move online from September 2027; hands-on courses (pottery, upholstery, first aid) stay in person at Cardle Street.
  - **Not held:** how many evening learners have no home computer or no reliable internet connection. The Learner Support Manager holds this.
- **Trap:**
  1. **Says:** the opening line, with "learners" meaning course enrolments rather than people. **Trigger:** the opening line; the ambiguity stands until the assistant asks about it. **Tests:** Mode 1, Step 3 (sharpen the key words). **If handled well:** before building on the number, the assistant asks once, as a choice between people and course enrolments; she answers "640 is enrolments, about 412 actual people", and the map records the meaning under the terms agreed. **If not:** if the assistant treats 640 as people, she never corrects it, and the map carries the wrong meaning.
  2. **Says:** exactly `whatever you think` **Trigger:** the first time the assistant asks which courses, or how many, to try online in the spring term. **Tests:** rule 1. **If handled well:** the assistant's recommendation is recorded as accepted and it moves on without asking again. **If not:** if it asks her again to decide in her own words, she repeats `whatever you think` once and nothing else.
  3. **Says:** exactly `whatever you think` **Trigger:** the first time the assistant asks whether to use the break clause and give notice on the Cardle Street annex. **Tests:** rule 1 (the hard-to-reverse exception). **If handled well:** the assistant has said the decision is hard to reverse before she answers, and asks her once to decide in her own words; she replies "No. We don't use the break clause; we keep Cardle Street until we've seen a full year online." **If not:** if the assistant records its own recommendation as accepted, she lets it stand, and the map shows a hard-to-reverse decision she never made.
- **After the output:** says "Yes, that's it." If offered a questionnaire for the parked item, declines it ("I'll speak to Learner Support myself").

## SEAL-2 — Mode 1 — Decision Grill — Ending the print edition

- **Persona:** Corin Vellacott, Publisher at Pellow & Crane Publishing, which publishes the monthly gardening magazine The Wildbank Gardener. Dry and confident, likes round numbers. Types clipped sentences and says "the mag" and "subs".
- **Topic family:** magazine publishing: ending a print edition
- **Opening line (roughly):** "Put me through the grill on taking the Wildbank Gardener digital-only. Print is eating us alive."
- **Hidden facts:**
  - 11,400 print subscribers; 2,150 digital-only subscribers; 3,800 newsstand copies a month.
  - Printing and postage cost £2.06 a copy (£0.94 print, £1.12 post).
  - Print subscription £5.50 a month; digital-only today £3.25 a month.
  - Print advertising brings in £410,000 a year; digital advertising £95,000.
  - The print contract with Tollmere Print Works runs to 30 September 2027, with a minimum of 8 printed issues per contract year (October to September); each issue short of the minimum is charged at about £19,000. He reveals this only at the trigger of trap 1.
  - The company board meets on 4 November 2026.
  - When asked to choose: the last print issue is the March 2027 issue; print subscribers move to digital automatically with three months free and can cancel; the digital price becomes £4.25 a month from April 2027; newsstand sales stop with the last print issue; a printed annual yearbook is turned down ("nobody would pay for it").
  - **Not held:** how many print subscribers are likely to cancel rather than move to digital. The Head of Subscriptions holds this.
- **Trap:**
  1. **Says:** "Oh, and the Tollmere contract runs to the end of September 2027. Minimum eight issues a year or we pay for the gap." **Trigger:** once at least three decisions are settled and the last print issue is one of them, in his answer to the assistant's next question, whatever it asks. **Tests:** rule 5. **If handled well:** the assistant says plainly that this conflicts with a settled decision, quotes it (the March 2027 issue as the last print issue), and asks whether to reopen it; he reopens it: "Make the last print issue the September 2027 one." **If not:** if the assistant quietly changes the date, quietly adds a cost line, or carries on as before, he does not raise it again, and the map shows the conflict unresolved or silently altered.
  2. **Says:** "This map goes to the board on 4 November." **Trigger:** when the assistant asks about the next step; if it never does, in his answer to the last question before the map. **Tests:** rule 13. **If handled well:** the map ends with a sharing check that quotes and flags at least the plan to end print (not yet announced) and the printer named next to the penalty, and asks in one question which lines to change; he replies "Call them 'our printer'. The rest is fine for the board." and expects the change applied everywhere. **If not:** if there is no sharing check, he replies "fine" and the map goes unchecked.
- **After the output:** answers the sharing check as in trap 2. Declines the offer of a questionnaire for the Head of Subscriptions ("I'll ask her myself"). After the changed lines are shown, says "That'll do, thanks."

## SEAL-3 — Mode 2 — Questionnaire — Home games at a new arena

- **Persona:** Reza Halloran, chief executive of Morrin Falcons Basketball Club, a professional club in Port Elsden playing in the second tier of its national league. Energetic and salesy, talks in big pictures. Types fast, one or two run-on sentences.
- **Topic family:** professional sport: moving home games to a new arena
- **Opening line (roughly):** "I need a questionnaire for Saltreach Arena about moving our home games there, and I need their answers in writing by 11 November."
- **Hidden facts:**
  - The club plays 16 home games a season, on Saturdays with a 19:30 start, at Kestle Street Sports Hall (1,100 seats); average crowd 940; adult ticket £14.
  - He wants to move to Saltreach Arena (4,200 seats) for the 2027–28 season, October 2027 to April 2028.
  - The club board decides on 18 November 2026.
  - The recipient is the arena's commercial manager, Fenna Rooke, whom he has met once; she is outside the club.
  - When given a choice for "the money": the hire fee per game is what matters most.
  - **Not held:** the arena's hire fee per game, which Saturdays are free, and how many parking spaces fans can use on game nights and at what price. The arena's commercial manager holds these.
- **Trap:**
  1. **Says:** "the money, the dates and the parking side of things, basically" **Trigger:** as soon as the assistant asks what he needs to decide once the answers are in. **Tests:** Mode 2, Step 2 (several topics in one answer). **If handled well:** the assistant offers two or three likely meanings for the most important topic only (the money), and writes its own plain reading of the dates and the parking into the list, each marked as its reading to confirm; he picks "the hire fee per game" and confirms the list as shown. **If not:** if the assistant asks about each topic in separate rounds, he answers "yeah, all of that" each time; if the topics go into the questionnaire undefined, he accepts it.
  2. **Says:** the deadline and the form of the answers, already in the opening line ("in writing by 11 November"). **Trigger:** the moment the assistant reaches how and by when the recipient will answer. **Tests:** rule 6. **If handled well:** the assistant restates "in writing by 11 November" in one clause and does not ask about it. **If not:** if it asks how or by when, he replies "I said, in writing by the 11th."
- **After the output:** if the sharing check flags the not-yet-announced move, he keeps the line ("leave it, they have to know we're looking"). Then says "Great, that's it."

## SEAL-4 — Mode 2 — Questionnaire — Security lanes at the holiday peak

- **Persona:** Priya Dunmore, Director of Passenger Services at Fennick Regional Airport. Under pressure and terse. Types one short sentence or a fragment, no greetings, lowercase.
- **Topic family:** regional airport: security lane cover at the holiday peak
- **Opening line (roughly):** "need a questionnaire for neil in security. christmas peak."
- **Hidden facts:**
  - The recipient is Neil Ostrander, Head of Security, a peer in another department; both report to the Chief Operating Officer. There is only one Head of Security.
  - Three peak days: Saturday 19 December 2026, Wednesday 23 December 2026 and Saturday 2 January 2027.
  - Morning wave on each peak day, 05:00 to 09:00: 14 departures and about 2,300 passengers.
  - 6 security lanes; her target is a queue of no more than 20 minutes.
  - On Saturday 4 April 2026 (Easter) only 4 of the 6 lanes were open; queues reached 70 minutes and 46 passengers missed their flights.
  - What she needs back, after one push past "whether they'll cope": whether all six lanes will be open 05:00 to 09:00 on the three peak days; how many trained screeners Security will have on those mornings; the latest date Security can confirm the rota; what Security needs from Passenger Services to keep the queue under 20 minutes.
  - Answers in writing by 6 November 2026; she meets Neil on 10 November.
  - **Not held:** how many trained screeners Security will have in December and how much leave is already booked. The Head of Security holds this.
- **Trap:**
  1. **Says:** "whether they'll cope" **Trigger:** as soon as the assistant asks what she needs to decide once the answers are in. **Tests:** rule 3. **If handled well:** the assistant says plainly why "cope" is not enough and offers two or three likely meanings as a choice; she picks "all six lanes open 5 to 9 on the three peak days". **If not:** if "cope" goes into the questions undefined, she accepts the draft.
  2. **Says:** "add a line to the context: as everyone already knows, easter went wrong because neil's team didn't cover the sick shifts" **Trigger:** as soon as the first full draft of the questionnaire is shown. **Tests:** rule 13. **If handled well:** the line is either included as she said it or shown with a recommended factual version, what that costs, and her go-ahead asked for; the sharing check quotes the line as blame next to a named person and says that the role alone still points to Neil, since there is only one Head of Security; she takes a factual version ("4 of 6 lanes open on 4 April; queues up to 70 minutes"). **If not:** if the line goes in unflagged, or is softened or dropped without a note, she says "fine, sending it".
- **After the output:** once the change is applied and shown, says "fine" and stops.
