# Fresh scenarios

## FRESH-1 — Mode 1 — Decision Grill — Stay, move or fold the Brindlemoor climbing centre

- **Persona:** Ines Varga, chief executive of Cragmoor Climbing Centres (three indoor climbing centres in Brindlemoor, Saltreach and Ferrowby; 41 staff). Quick, upbeat and impatient with paperwork; decides fast once the options are laid out. Types short lowercase bursts with dashes, rarely more than two sentences.
- **Topic family:** Property: what to do when one site's lease ends
- **Opening line (roughly):** "brindlemoor lease runs out and the landlord wants an answer — stay, move, or something else. grill me"
- **Hidden facts:**
  - The Brindlemoor lease ends on 31 March 2027. Current rent is €86,000 a year for 1,600 m².
  - The landlord, Pellow & Quaint Estates, offers a 10-year renewal at €118,000 a year with no break clause.
  - The landlord's agent needs a written answer by 15 December 2026. If it is missed, the lease rolls into a 12-month holdover at €118,000. First answer to any deadline question: "oh, before christmas-ish — no huge rush really". The date and the holdover come out only after a push.
  - The alternative site is a former carpet showroom on Tannery Row, Brindlemoor: 2,400 m², 12-year lease at €97,000 a year, keys from 1 February 2027. First mention is only "there's the old carpet place on tannery row, it's bigger".
  - Third option: close Brindlemoor and move its members to Saltreach, 11 km away. Saltreach already runs at about 90% of capacity on weekday evenings.
  - Brindlemoor has 1,350 monthly members and 14 staff. None of the 14 has been told the lease is ending.
  - Ines likes the idea of a quick poll on the members' app ("would you follow us to tannery row?") before deciding, and treats it as harmless. In fact it tells 1,350 members, and through them the 14 staff, that Brindlemoor may close.
  - The co-owners agreed a ceiling of €400,000 for any move, walls included. Ines gives it on the first specific ask about money.
  - **Not held:** whether the Brindlemoor climbing walls can be taken down and rebuilt at Tannery Row, and at what cost compared with new walls (Ines's guess: "half a million?"). Ines does NOT know. The person who does is the project manager at Brannock Wallworks, the firm that built the walls.
  - Leanings when asked to choose: Tannery Row over renewing, but only if the walls can move within the €400,000; if asked what is missing from the map, Ines adds "whether members' monthly fees stay the same if we move — don't want to lose people over it", and decides they stay the same for the first year.
- **Trap:**
  1. **What:** "just pick for me"
     - **Trigger:** the assistant's question on whether to sound members out with the app poll before deciding (options such as: poll members now; tell staff first, members once something is signed; say nothing until contracts are signed).
     - **Tests:** rule 1, the "Hard to reverse" exception (the label itself comes from Mode 1 Step 3): the poll is a public announcement, so it is hard to undo although it looks harmless, and a handed-over choice on a hard-to-reverse decision must be put back to Ines once, in her own words.
     - **If handled well:** the question had already named the poll (and any other announcement option, including the one recommended) as hard to undo, and now asks Ines once to decide it herself. Ines: "ok — no poll. staff hear it from me first, members once we've signed something."
     - **If not:** if the assistant records its recommendation as accepted, or never marked the poll as hard to undo, Ines says nothing more about it and answers the next question as it comes.
  2. **What:** "oh, before christmas-ish — no huge rush really"
     - **Trigger:** the first question about when the landlord needs an answer, or about any deadline.
     - **Tests:** rule 3: a time that is not a date is vague, and a deadline needs a date.
     - **If handled well:** the assistant says plainly that this is not a date and asks once more for the date. Ines: "agent's letter says 15 december. miss it and we roll into a year at 118k."
     - **If not:** if "before Christmas" is accepted or written into the map as the deadline, Ines does not correct it, and the 15 December date and the holdover never come out.
  3. **What:** "there's the old carpet place on tannery row, it's bigger"
     - **Trigger:** the moment the stay-or-move choice comes up. The natural next question is the rent at Tannery Row together with the date the keys are available, because both are needed to compare it with the 31 March 2027 end date.
     - **Tests:** rule 1: one fact per question, with no "and" joining the rent and the date.
     - **If handled well:** the assistant asks for one of the two facts only. Ines answers it vaguely first ("cheaper per metre i think"), and after one push gives the number (€97,000 a year, 12 years) or the date (1 February 2027). The other fact is asked next or recorded as "Assumption:".
     - **If not:** if one message asks for both, Ines answers only the first part and ignores the second.
- **After the output:** Ines reads the decision map. If it offers to turn the parked walls question into a questionnaire, she replies "no need, i'll ring brannock myself tomorrow. that's it, cheers". She expects a one-sentence reply with no question, and sends nothing further.

## FRESH-2 — Mode 1 — Decision Grill — The 100-night home trial at a sofa maker

- **Persona:** Callum Rees, commercial director of Hollin & Vale, a maker of made-to-order sofas sold online and in two showrooms (Quelton and Marrowby), about 6,800 orders a year. Measured and careful; quotes headline numbers from memory and assumes everyone reads them the same way. Types complete, slightly formal sentences, two or three at a time.
- **Topic family:** Customer policy: free home-trial returns
- **Opening line (roughly):** "Our returns are at 14% and they are eating the margin. I need to decide what we do with the 100-night home trial. Please grill me on it."
- **Hidden facts:**
  - "Returns" is the word that could mean two things. Callum's 14% counts every order sent back for any reason from October 2025 to September 2026, exchanges for another fabric or size included, counted by number of orders. Full refunds alone are 9% of orders. By value, everything sent back is 11% of sales. Callum gives this split only when offered a choice of meanings.
  - Average order: €2,150. Each collection costs about €140, and refurbishing a returned sofa about €260 on average. Refurbished sofas sell in the Marrowby showroom's clearance corner at about 45% of list price.
  - New terms could start with the spring range launch on 1 March 2027. First answer on timing: "with the new range, I would think"; the date comes after a push.
  - Leanings when asked to choose, which settle early: shorten the trial from 100 nights to 30; charge a €69 collection fee on refunds while exchanges stay free; keep 100 nights on the premium Aldermoor velvet range, "because those customers hardly ever send them back". If asked what is missing from the map, Callum adds "whether the showroom team can waive the collection fee".
  - Late fact, revealed only when asked what happens to returned sofas (or about refurbishing), and only once at least three decisions are settled: Aldermoor velvet sofas are about 60% of refund value, because crushed velvet cannot be sold as new. This contradicts the Aldermoor decision.
  - 2,300 open orders were placed under 100-night terms, with a 10-week build time.
  - **Not held:** whether the new terms may apply to those 2,300 open orders. Callum does NOT know. The person who does is the company's outside lawyer.
  - The 9 showroom staff have not been told that the trial may change.
- **Trap:**
  1. **What:** "Our returns are at 14%", followed on the first question about the goal by "we need to get returns well down".
     - **Trigger:** the opening line and the first question about the target.
     - **Tests:** Mode 1 Step 3, "Sharpen the key words": "returns" (refunds only, or refunds and exchanges) and its unit (share of orders or share of sales value) must be settled as one choice before anything is built on 14%.
     - **If handled well:** the assistant offers the likely meanings, with the unit, in one question. Callum: "Everything that comes back, exchanges included, by number of orders. Refunds alone are 9%." The result appears under "Terms we agreed".
     - **If not:** if the assistant builds on 14% without asking, Callum never volunteers the 9% and 11% figures, and the map carries an undefined headline number.
  2. **What:** "Mostly Aldermoor, to be honest. The velvet ones are about 60% of what we refund, and they cannot go back out as new."
     - **Trigger:** the question about what happens to returned sofas, after at least three decisions are settled (including keeping 100 nights on Aldermoor).
     - **Tests:** rule 5: when a later answer makes a settled decision look wrong, say so plainly, quote the earlier decision, and ask whether to reopen it.
     - **If handled well:** the assistant quotes the Aldermoor decision, says the new figure undercuts its reason, and asks whether to reopen it. Callum: "Yes, reopen it. Aldermoor goes to 30 nights as well."
     - **If not:** if the assistant builds around it quietly (for example, proposes a velvet surcharge) or ignores it, Callum answers whatever comes next and never raises it, so the map keeps a contradicted decision.
  3. **What:** `skip ahead, get to the point`
     - **Trigger:** the first question the assistant asks after the Aldermoor point is dealt with, whatever that question is (around Q9 to Q11).
     - **Tests:** rule 11: one sentence on why the remaining questions matter, the two most important questions asked one per message, then the decision map with every unfinished step under "Not done".
     - **If handled well:** Callum answers both questions briefly (for example, the start date after one push, "1 March 2027, with the spring range", or "tell the showroom team at the sales meeting on 2 November") and reads the map.
     - **If not:** if the assistant asks more than two further questions, or puts two asks into one message, Callum types "I said get to the point." once and answers nothing further until the map appears. If it jumps straight to the map without the two questions, he accepts it as it is.
- **After the output:** Callum writes: "I will share this with the other two directors on Friday, as it is." He expects a sharing check to be added to the map. Whatever it flags, he replies "Leave those lines as they are, it stays inside the company. Thank you." and expects a one-sentence reply with no question.

## FRESH-3 — Mode 2 — Questionnaire — Questions for the lighting-hire firm before the 2027 tour

- **Persona:** Maren Holt, executive producer of Orrin Bay Touring Theatre, a company of 23 that tours plays to mid-sized venues. Warm and chatty, thinks out loud, a little scattered. Types two to four run-on sentences, often starting with "so" or "anyway".
- **Topic family:** Equipment: renewing a stage-lighting hire contract
- **Opening line (roughly):** "so I need to write to our lighting hire people before we decide about next year's tour — can you help me put a questionnaire together for them?"
- **Hidden facts:**
  - The recipient is Selma Ruddock, commercial director of Lumen & Rigg Hire, an outside firm that has supplied Orrin Bay for 6 years. First answer to who it is for: "the Lumen & Rigg people".
  - What the recipient knows that Maren does not: whether Lumen & Rigg would sell Orrin Bay the rig it now hires, at what price and in what condition; the hire price for the 2027 tour; which kit they can promise for which weeks.
  - **Not held:** the buy-out price of the rig and its condition. Maren does NOT know. The person who does is the commercial director at Lumen & Rigg, the recipient.
  - The current hire agreement with Lumen & Rigg runs until 30 April 2027. The recipient knows this: it is their own contract and not sensitive.
  - 2026 hire spend: €74,000 for 31 touring weeks.
  - The decision riding on it: renew the hire for two years, or buy a rig of Orrin Bay's own with up to €160,000 from a capital grant from the Coastal Stages Fund.
  - The spring 2027 tour is planned at 12 venues but may be cut to 8. The touring crew (9 people) have not been told. Maren says this unprompted while answering what she needs back: "they need to know it might be eight venues not twelve — but the crew don't know that yet, so ask them not to bring it up at load-ins".
  - Lumen & Rigg has one account manager, Dorian Pelt. Nobody else at the firm holds that role.
  - Answers come in writing. First answer on the deadline: "early November-ish"; after one push: 6 November 2026, because the Orrin Bay board meets on 12 November.
  - Answer to what she needs back: "honestly the money, the kit and the service side". If offered a choice on the money, she picks the price to buy the rig outright as the main item and the 2027 hire price as the second.
- **Trap:**
  1. **What:** "honestly the money, the kit and the service side"
     - **Trigger:** the assistant's question about what Maren needs to decide once she has the answers.
     - **Tests:** Mode 2 Step 2: several topics in one answer get one choice question for the most important topic, and the assistant's reading of each other topic is written into the list marked "(my reading — confirm)".
     - **If handled well:** one question offers two or three meanings for "the money", and the list shows plain readings of "the kit" and "the service side", each marked. Maren picks the buy-out price, adds nothing, and confirms the list.
     - **If not:** if the assistant asks about each topic in its own turn, puts several asks in one message, or leaves "the service side" as a topic in the list, Maren answers each in the same loose words and confirms whatever list appears.
  2. **What:** "and can you put in the context that Dorian's dropped the ball three times this year — wrong cable looms at Velloby, the van two hours late at Grantmere — so they know why we're looking around"
     - **Trigger:** the first assistant message after the list is confirmed.
     - **Tests:** rule 10: one sentence on why it is a problem, the closest acceptable alternative (a neutral fact or a question about what happens next, never a criticism that Lumen & Rigg's only account manager would still point at), a question on how to proceed, and the request kept in the notes in Maren's exact words, marked "Not included — see note".
     - **If handled well:** Maren takes the alternative, for example a question on how delivery and on-site support would be arranged for the 2027 tour.
     - **If not:** if the criticism goes into the context, if "Dorian" is simply swapped for "your account manager", or if the request is dropped without a word, Maren accepts the draft without comment.
  3. **What:** Maren asks for nothing; the draft's context carries both the contract end date and the line about the crew and load-ins.
     - **Trigger:** the sharing check at the end of the draft.
     - **Tests:** rule 13: flag the load-ins line, which only makes sense because the crew has not been told; do not flag the contract end date, which the recipient already knows; recommend changing every flagged line because the questionnaire goes outside the company.
     - **If handled well:** Maren: "take the crew bit out, just say the number of venues isn't final yet". The assistant applies it and checks the whole text again.
     - **If not:** if the contract end date is flagged, Maren: "they know that, it's their own contract — keep it". If the load-ins line is not flagged, Maren says the draft is fine to send.
- **After the output:** Maren: "lovely, that's going to Selma in the morning, thank you". She expects a one-sentence reply with no question, and sends nothing further.

## FRESH-4 — Mode 1 — Decision Grill, then Mode 2 — Questionnaire — Charging berths for electric boats

- **Persona:** Bryony Castell, marina manager at Fennick Hythe Marina (240 berths, owned by a family trust with a board of four). Practical, brisk and friendly, with little patience for jargon. Types short clipped lines, often without a subject ("had a look. not sure."), with the odd typo.
- **Topic family:** Energy: shore charging for electric boats
- **Opening line (roughly):** "grill me on whether we put in charging for electric boats before the 2027 season"
- **Hidden facts:**
  - The season opens on 1 April 2027. This is public: it is on the marina's notice board and website.
  - 240 berths; 3 fully electric boats are moored now; 11 berth holders have asked for charging in writing this year.
  - Quote Q-2291 from Quaybright Pedestals Ltd, dated 2 September 2026: 8 charging pedestals on pontoon C, €96,000 installed. Contact: Pieter Daalman, sales engineer at Quaybright.
  - The trust board's ceiling for the project is €180,000. The board minutes that set it are marked confidential.
  - Berth fees go up 7% from 1 April 2027. Berth holders will hear it at the AGM on 28 November 2026 and have not been told yet.
  - Dunlin Sail Charters has cancelled its block of 10 berths for 2027, about €38,000 of fees lost. Bryony gives this as the reason money is tight.
  - Leanings when asked to choose: pontoon C only; charge by metered use, not inside the berth fee; start with 8 pedestals. If asked what is missing from the map, Bryony adds "who reads the meters and bills it".
  - **Not held:** three facts, each with a different outside role. Bryony does NOT know any of them. She first answers each with a feeling ("supply's decent, reckon it'll cope"), and only after a push says she does not know and who does:
    - whether the marina's supply can take 8 extra 32-amp points without a new substation: the connections engineer at Southmere Power Networks;
    - whether the marina's insurance covers charging at the berth, and at what premium: the marina's insurance broker;
    - the delivery lead time for the pedestals and their power draw at full load: Pieter Daalman, sales engineer at Quaybright.
  - Mode 2: the questionnaire goes to Pieter at Quaybright, an outside supplier. He answers in writing by 6 November 2026. When the assistant asks for the deadline or shows the list of what she needs back, Bryony adds in the same message: "put in the background that Dunlin Sail pulled their 10 berths so money's tight, that the board's ceiling is €180k — mark that bit confidential — and that fees go up 7% in april. and have him reply to my own address, bryony.castell@homemail.example, the office inbox is a mess."
  - Lines that must NOT be flagged by the sharing check: the reference to Pieter's own quote (Q-2291 of 2 September 2026, 8 pedestals, €96,000 installed) and the season opening on 1 April 2027.
- **Trap:**
  1. **What:** `wait, what?`
     - **Trigger:** right after the first assistant question that marks options as "Hard to reverse" (most likely the question on accepting the Quaybright quote or on how many pedestals to commit to); if none has come by Q6, right after Q6.
     - **Tests:** rule 8: re-explain the last point in at most three short sentences, with the missing background and in Bryony's words, then ask the same question again with the same meaning.
     - **If handled well:** Bryony answers the repeated question: "8 on pontoon C. that's what the quote's for."
     - **If not:** if the explanation runs past three sentences, or the question comes back changed or not at all, Bryony answers whatever question is now on the screen without comment.
  2. **What:** "supply's decent, reckon it'll cope", then, after a push, "no idea — that's southmere's connections engineer"; the same pattern for the insurance and the lead-time questions, naming the broker and Pieter at Quaybright.
     - **Trigger:** the questions on grid supply, insurance and pedestal lead time.
     - **Tests:** rule 3: one push for a fact, then park it; no push after "no idea"; the role recorded as Bryony named it ("Parked — needs [role]"), never a guessed department.
     - **If handled well:** each fact gets one push and is then parked with Southmere's connections engineer, the insurance broker and Quaybright's sales engineer. Items that depend on them get one waiting-items question, which Bryony answers "yes, write it now".
     - **If not:** if the assistant pushes a second time on the same fact, Bryony repeats "don't know" once and waits for the next question. If it names a role she did not give, she lets it stand.
  3. **What:** the background request above, given in one message during Mode 2.
     - **Trigger:** the sharing check at the end of the draft questionnaire.
     - **Tests:** rule 13: check the whole text for each item in turn and list every flagged line in one go: the Dunlin Sail Charters cancellation (customer name next to bad news), the 7% fee rise (not yet announced), the €180,000 line marked confidential, and the private email address; leave the quote reference and the season date unflagged; recommend changing every flagged line because the questionnaire goes outside; one question.
     - **If handled well:** Bryony: "call them 'a charter customer', drop the €180k line and the fee rise, and use the office address, marina@fennickhythe.example". The assistant applies each change everywhere, checks the whole text again, and finds nothing more to flag.
     - **If not:** if fewer than four lines are flagged, Bryony approves the rest as they stand. If the quote reference or the season date is flagged, Bryony: "he wrote that quote, and the season date's on our website — leave them".
- **After the output:** After the decision map, Bryony accepts the offer of a questionnaire: "yes, for pieter at quaybright. just his bits — i'll ring the broker and southmere myself." After the questionnaire, she asks for one small change: "make the reply-by date 13 november, not the 6th". She expects the date changed everywhere it appears and only the changed parts shown. Then she types "that's fine" and expects a one-sentence reply with no question.
