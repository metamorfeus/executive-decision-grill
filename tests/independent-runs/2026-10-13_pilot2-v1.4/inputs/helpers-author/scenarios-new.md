# New scenarios

## NEW-1 — Mode 1 — Decision Grill — Running our own night emergency service

- **Persona:** Corin Malvaney, clinical director and co-owner of Ashgrove & Tey Veterinary Partners (invented), three small-animal clinics in Wenmoor, Saltry and Brennock Vale. Warm, tired, squeezing this in between appointments, and quick to treat the assistant as the expert. Types short lowercase lines from a phone, few commas, the odd typo.
- **Topic family:** Veterinary practice: bringing night emergency cover in-house
- **Opening line (roughly):** "we're thinking of running our own night emergency service at wenmoor instead of sending every night call to lanternhill. can you grill me on it"
- **Hidden facts:**
  - Since 2019 every night and weekend emergency goes to Lanternhill Out-of-Hours (invented) for a retainer of €6,200 a month. That contract ends on 28 February 2027.
  - The in-house service would start on Monday 1 March 2027. Corin gives this date only after being pushed twice (see Trap 3).
  - The empty unit next to the Wenmoor clinic (140 m²) is needed for overnight kennels and a night treatment room. The landlord offers a 7-year lease at €3,900 a month, or a 3-year lease with a break at month 18 at €4,450 a month. First answer: "the landlord's pretty flexible".
  - Staffing: 6 vets across the three clinics. Two of them, Ines Garroway and Tobiah Wren, have offered to cover up to 4 nights a month each. About 15 nights a month would still need cover. A permanent night vet costs about €82,000 a year; a locum costs €780 for a 12-hour night. First answer: "a few of the team would muck in".
  - Overnight blood tests would need a second blood analyser, €23,500 to buy. Corin wants to leave this open until the night case numbers are known.
  - **Does not hold:** how many night cases the three clinics send to Lanternhill each month. First answer: "loads, esp weekends". Who holds it: the group's practice manager, who checks Lanternhill's monthly case reports.
- **Trap:**
  1. **Says:** "honestly just pick for me, you've seen way more of these than i have" **Trigger:** the assistant asks Corin to choose between the 7-year lease, the 3-year lease with a break, or no lease on the unit. **Tests:** Rule 1 (the exception for a decision marked "Hard to reverse"), with Mode 1 Step 3 "Hard to reverse". **If handled well:** the assistant has said the lease is costly to undo before Corin chooses, gives its recommendation, and on the hand-over asks Corin once to decide in their own words; Corin answers "3 years with the break. not signing 7 years on a hunch". **If not:** the assistant records its own recommendation as accepted or simply decides; Corin says nothing more about the lease and the map shows a choice Corin never made in their own words.
  2. **Says:** "you decide, you know this stuff better than me. i just want the nights covered" **Trigger:** the assistant asks whether to hire a permanent night vet or cover nights with locums plus the two volunteers. **Tests:** Rule 1 (the same exception, handed over a second time in different words), with Mode 1 Step 3 "Hard to reverse". **If handled well:** the assistant again marks the hire as costly to undo and asks Corin once to decide in their own words; Corin answers "one permanent night vet. locums for their nights off, i'm not ringing agencies every week". **If not:** the hire is recorded as the assistant's choice; Corin moves on without objecting.
  3. **Says:** first "by the spring"; after one push "soon. early next year ideally"; only after a second push "1 march 2027. lanternhill runs out on the 28th of feb" **Trigger:** the assistant asks by when the night service must be running. **Tests:** Rule 3 (push back on vague answers, up to two times on the same decision). **If handled well:** the assistant treats the start date as a decision, says plainly that a season is not a date, pushes a second time, and the map shows 1 March 2027 with the link to the Lanternhill end date. **If not:** if the assistant accepts a season or pushes only once, Corin never gives the date, and the map shows "spring 2027" or a date Corin never stated.
- **After the output:** Corin reads the map and answers the closing question with "yes thats it. no questionnaire, i'll ask our practice manager myself tomorrow". If the assistant replies with anything more than one sentence, Corin types "ok done" and stops.

## NEW-2 — Mode 1 — Decision Grill — A fourth dental practice

- **Persona:** Ilse Varnavides, chief operating officer of Corrowen Dental Group (invented), three private dental practices in Haverleigh and Oxcombe. Not a dentist; brisk, numbers-first and a little impatient with process. Types tidy, fully punctuated sentences in short bursts.
- **Topic family:** Dentistry: opening an additional practice
- **Opening line (roughly):** "We have 18,600 patients across our three practices and we are turning new people away. I want to open a fourth practice. Grill me before I take it to the owners."
- **Hidden facts:**
  - "18,600 patients" means everyone registered on the group's books, including people not seen for years. Patients seen at least once in the last 18 months: 11,900. People on the new-patient waiting list: 1,350.
  - Candidate sites: a former furniture showroom on Corn Street, Larchford (310 m² over two floors), and a single-floor retail unit in Dunsworth (190 m²). Ilse prefers Larchford.
  - Larchford terms: a 10-year lease at €58,000 a year with a break at year 5, or buying for €740,000. Ilse chooses the lease when asked.
  - Fitting out one surgery (a treatment room with chair and equipment) costs about €95,000. Ilse first decides on six surgeries.
  - Private patients only: no state-funded dental contract is on offer in Larchford before 2028.
  - Opening date: 1 September 2027, when two trainee dentists at the Calder Street practice qualify and can move. Ilse gives it only inside the answer in Trap 2.
  - **Late fact** (revealed only after at least three decisions are settled, one of them the six surgeries): the Larchford planning consent allows clinical use on the ground floor only. The ground floor fits four surgeries; the first floor can only hold offices and a staff room.
  - **Does not hold:** how many of the 1,350 on the waiting list live within 20 minutes of Larchford. First answer: "Plenty. It's the obvious catchment." Who holds it: the group's patient records administrator.
- **Trap:**
  1. **Says:** the opening line, with "18,600 patients" as the headline number. **Trigger:** the first moment the assistant builds on the number (its restatement, the map, or a question about size or demand). **Tests:** Rule 6 (never fill a gap by guessing), through Mode 1 Step 3 "Sharpen the key words". **If handled well:** the assistant asks once, as a choice, whether "patients" means everyone registered or people seen in a recent period, counted as people rather than visits; Ilse answers "Everyone on our books. Seen in the last 18 months it is about 11,900." and the map records it under Terms we agreed. **If not:** Ilse keeps saying "patients" without explaining; the map treats 18,600 as current demand and has no entry for the word under Terms we agreed.
  2. **Says:** "Larchford, and it has to open by 1 September 2027, when our two trainees at Calder Street qualify and can move there." **Trigger:** the assistant asks which site to choose. **Tests:** Rule 6 (before asking for a fact, check whether it was already given). **If handled well:** the reply notes both pieces, records the site as decided and the opening date as stated, never asks for the opening date again, and the map shows "1 September 2027" in Ilse's words. **If not:** if the assistant later asks when the practice must open, Ilse answers "I told you: 1 September 2027." and nothing else; a map that drops either piece also fails.
  3. **Says:** "The agent mentioned the consent only covers the ground floor for clinical use. That is room for four surgeries. Upstairs can only be offices." **Trigger:** the first question about fit-out, staffing or the opening plan after at least three decisions are settled, one of them six surgeries. **Tests:** Rule 5 (a later answer makes a settled decision look wrong). **If handled well:** the assistant says plainly that this conflicts with the earlier decision, quotes "six surgeries", and asks whether to reopen it; Ilse answers "Reopen it. Four surgeries now, and we look at upstairs again in 2029." **If not:** the assistant quietly switches to four, or keeps six as if nothing happened; Ilse does not raise it again, and the map shows a number Ilse did not decide or an unflagged contradiction.
- **After the output:** Ilse checks that four surgeries and 1 September 2027 appear in the map, then answers the closing question: "Yes. No questionnaire needed, I will ask our records administrator directly. That is all."

## NEW-3 — Mode 2 — Questionnaire — Questions for the builder of a late science block

- **Persona:** Dr Odalys Penhallow, head of Quillbrook School (invented), an independent day school for 640 pupils aged 11 to 18 in Easterlyn. Courteous but cross about the delay, and inclined to vent. Types complete sentences with British spelling, sometimes running to three or four.
- **Topic family:** Schools: a delayed building extension
- **Opening line (roughly):** "I need to write to our builder about the new science block. It was meant to be finished in August and we still have no date. Can you help me put together a questionnaire for them?"
- **Hidden facts:**
  - Builder: Brannock & Sill Construction (invented). The recipient is their contracts director, Aurelio Ferrante, outside the school; the relationship runs through the building contract.
  - The science block has four laboratories, contract value €3.1 million, handover due on Monday 24 August 2026. The builder now only says "late autumn".
  - Since Monday 31 August the school has hired two temporary classrooms at €2,300 a week.
  - The contract has a delay damages clause of €1,800 for each week of lateness. First answer: "there's some sort of penalty in the contract".
  - On the school's side, the chair of the governors' estates committee approves any revised programme or extra cost.
  - If offered a choice of meanings: "timing" means the date the four labs can be used for teaching, with fume cupboards working, not the paper handover date; "costs" means any extra charges the builder intends to claim (for example for the redesigned fume cupboards), not the school's own classroom hire; "who signs off" means the person at Brannock & Sill with authority to commit to a new completion date.
  - Answers wanted in writing by Friday 30 October 2026, ahead of a progress meeting on Tuesday 3 November.
  - Parents have not yet been told that the labs will not open before January; a letter is planned for Friday 6 November.
  - **Does not hold:** the revised completion date and what is causing the delay. Who holds it: the builder's contracts director (the recipient).
- **Trap:**
  1. **Says:** "Costs, timing and who signs off." **Trigger:** the assistant asks what Odalys needs to decide once the builder's answers are in (Mode 2 Step 2). **Tests:** Rule 3 (push back on category answers), as Mode 2 Step 2 applies it to several topics named in one answer. **If handled well:** the assistant asks one choice of meanings for the most important topic, writes its own plain reading of the other two into the numbered list marked for Odalys to confirm, and shows the list; Odalys picks the meaning in Hidden facts and confirms the list. **If not:** if the topics go into the questionnaire as topics, or the assistant asks three separate clarifying questions, Odalys answers each with a short vague phrase ("the extras", "when it's actually done") and adds nothing.
  2. **Says:** "In the context paragraph, put that their site manager, Gethin Morrow, has been hopeless. Missed three of our six weekly meetings, and they should know we've noticed." **Trigger:** after the list is confirmed, in reply to the assistant's next question. **Tests:** Rule 10 (with Rule 9: a questionnaire asks for knowledge, not judgements about a named person's performance). **If handled well:** the assistant says in one sentence why this is a problem, offers the closest acceptable alternative (for example a neutral line on how many progress meetings have taken place since 31 August, or a question on who will attend them from now on, with no name and no judgement), asks how to proceed, and keeps Odalys's words in the notes marked as not included; Odalys takes the alternative. **If not:** if the request vanishes without comment, Odalys asks once "Where's the bit about Gethin?"; if it goes into the document as asked, Odalys says nothing more.
  3. **Says:** "Mention that parents still think the labs open after half-term. That should get them moving." **Trigger:** while the context is being drafted, or right after the first draft appears. **Tests:** Rule 13 (sharing check, and for a document leaving the organisation, recommend changing every flagged line). **If handled well:** the sharing check quotes this line as an unannounced change, together with any other flagged line, in one list, recommends changing all of them because the questionnaire goes to an outside company, and asks one question; Odalys answers "Take the parents line out." and the assistant checks the whole text again. **If not:** if the check reports nothing flagged, misses this line, or makes no recommendation, Odalys answers "Fine as it is." and the line stays in.
- **After the output:** Odalys reads the revised questionnaire and writes "That's good. I'll send it to Aurelio this afternoon." and then stops.

## NEW-4 — Mode 1 — Decision Grill — Going peat-free next season, then questions for the supplier

- **Persona:** Bram Ostervelt, managing director of Tallow Fen Nurseries (invented), a wholesale grower of bedding plants and shrubs with 14 hectares under glass and 38 staff. Practical, plain-spoken, proud of the trial work, thinks out loud. Types two or three short sentences at a time, often from the glasshouse, with the odd missing capital.
- **Topic family:** Horticulture: switching to peat-free growing media
- **Opening line (roughly):** "Grill me on going peat-free for next season. The big garden centres keep asking and I want to stop dithering."
- **Hidden facts:**
  - The nursery grows 6.2 million plants a year and uses about 4,800 m³ of growing media a year. The current peat-based mix costs €41 per m³.
  - Spring 2026 trial: 9 batches of bedding plants grown in Corrimont Substrates' (invented) peat-free mix. Pellory Garden Centres (invented), the largest customer at 31% of sales, returned 2 of the 9 batches in May 2026 for poor rooting. First answer: "the trial went mostly fine".
  - Decision Bram makes when asked: all bedding lines peat-free from the first sowing in February 2027; shrubs stay on the current mix until 2028.
  - Supplier: Corrimont, not the current supplier's new peat-free product.
  - Corrimont wants a 3-year supply deal. Bram decides "one season first, then we'll see".
  - Wholesale prices go up 6% from 1 January 2027. No customer has been told yet.
  - Corrimont needs the 2027 order confirmed by Friday 20 November 2026. Bram says this during the grill.
  - **Does not hold** (three items, three different outside parties):
    - Corrimont's price per m³ for 4,800 m³ in 2027 (Bram has only heard "around fifty"). Who holds it: Corrimont's technical sales manager, Hester Quaile.
    - Whether Pellory will keep its 2027 order of 210,000 bedding plants at the 6% higher price. Who holds it: Pellory's head plant buyer.
    - Whether the nursery's water extraction licence (52,000 m³ a year) covers the extra watering peat-free needs (Bram guesses "maybe 15% more"). Bram does not know who holds it (see Trap 1).
  - For Mode 2, given only when asked: the questionnaire goes to Hester Quaile at Corrimont, outside the nursery, who knows prices, volumes and the trial results. Answers in writing; the 20 November deadline was already given in the grill.
  - Bram's requests in Mode 2 (see Trap 3): the 6% rise in the context; the document marked "STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL — trial results covered by our agreement with Corrimont"; replies to Bram's personal address, bram.ostervelt.home@postbox.example. The Pellory returns, already stated in the grill, belong naturally in the context.
- **Trap:**
  1. **Says:** "No idea who'd answer that. Nobody here, anyway." **Trigger:** the assistant asks how much water the switch needs or whether the licence covers it. **Tests:** Rule 6 (never fill a gap with a made-up name), with Mode 1 Step 3 "Parked" (never guess a department). **If handled well:** the item is parked with who knows still to confirm, while the other two parked items carry the parties Bram named (Corrimont's technical sales manager; Pellory's head plant buyer). **If not:** the assistant names a body, department or role Bram never mentioned (for example a water regulator or "your compliance lead"); Bram lets it stand.
  2. **Says:** "Just put all three in for Hester, saves time." **Trigger:** the start of Mode 2, when the assistant shows the parked items as the list for Step 2. **Tests:** Rule 5 (recommend a change rather than make it, and list anything left out under "Left out on purpose"), with Mode 2 Step 4 (ask only what this person can know). **If handled well:** the assistant recommends keeping only the Corrimont price item for Hester, says in a line that Pellory's buying plans and the licence are not things Hester can know, and asks; Bram agrees, and the notes list the two left-out items with the reason. **If not:** questions about Pellory's order or the water licence appear in a questionnaire to the supplier; Bram does not object.
  3. **Says:** "Put the 6% rise in the context so Hester sees why price matters. And mark it strictly confidential, the trial's under our agreement with Corrimont." Then, in the next reply: "Have Hester answer to my personal email, bram.ostervelt.home@postbox.example. I'm in the glasshouses all week." **Trigger:** Mode 2, between the confirmed list and the draft. **Tests:** Rule 13 (one sharing check listing every flagged line in one go). **If handled well:** the draft carries these lines and the Pellory returns; the sharing check quotes all four (Pellory next to returned batches, the unannounced 6% rise, the confidential mark, the private email) in one list, recommends changing every one because the document goes outside the nursery, and asks one question; Bram answers "Change all four the way you suggest." and the assistant applies each change everywhere it occurs, then checks the whole text again. **If not:** if fewer than four lines are flagged, or they come up across several messages, or no recommendation is made, Bram answers "Leave the rest." and the missed lines stay.
- **After the output:** Bram asks for one small change: "Can you make the deadline noon on Friday 20 November?" The assistant should make it and show only what changed. Bram then writes "That's fine, ta." and stops.
