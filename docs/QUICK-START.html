Executive Decision Grill — quick start

A printable guide to setting up the Claude Project instructions and starting your first decision interview.

Free Claude Project instructions for CEOs, CFOs, COOs, directors and managers, built to question a business decision, produce a decision map and draft a questionnaire for whoever holds the missing answers—with nothing to install.

Please note. Version 1.5 has been tested only in simulated conversations, not yet in the real Claude app. Read every document Claude writes before you use or send it.

Set it up once (5 minutes)

  1. Open Claude (the desktop app or claude.ai; a computer is easier than a phone for the paste). Open Projects, click + New Project and name it Executive Decision Grill.
  2. Go to metamorfeus.github.io/executive-decision-grill and click Copy the instructions. (It is the whole file PASTE-INTO-CLAUDE.txt.)
  3. In the Project, choose Set project instructions (on some screens: Instructions), paste, and click Save instructions.
  4. Start a new chat inside the Project for each decision. If it worked, Claude restates your decision in one sentence and lists the smaller decisions inside it.

Anthropic's help pages say Projects are available on every plan, including Free (at most five Projects), and that you can set instructions for each Project. We have not tried it on a Free account, so check that the Set project instructions button appears.

The two modes

1 · Decision Grill

"Grill me on whether we should move customer service to an outside provider."

  • Claude lists the smaller decisions hiding inside yours. You add what is missing.
  • It asks one question at a time, each with My recommendation:. "Agreed" is a full answer.
  • It checks words that could mean two things, and offers three different options when you're stuck.

You get: a decision map. Decided · What we said no to · Parked with someone else · Open · Not yet clear · Terms we agreed · Assumptions · Next step.

2 · Questionnaire

"Write a questionnaire for our head of IT about the warehouse go-live."

  • Claude asks only who it is for, what they know that you don't, and what you need back.
  • It drafts neutral questions, one idea each, most important first.
  • A sharing check flags names next to criticism, unannounced plans and personal data.

You get: a questionnaire ready to paste into an email or a document.

Useful phrases

SayWhat happens
AgreedYou accept Claude's recommended answer and move on.
wait, what?Claude re-explains its last point in three short sentences, then asks again.
I don't knowThe item is parked with the person who would know, or left open. Claude is built not to make up an answer.
let's wrap upStraight to the decision map, with unfinished items listed.
skip the questionsClaude asks the two most important questions, then writes the output.

Good habits

  • Give numbers, names and dates. If you answer with a category or a feeling, Claude is built to ask again. That is the point.
  • Big, hard-to-undo decisions stay yours. If you say "whatever you think" on a multi-year contract, Claude is built to ask you to decide it yourself.
  • Finish one decision in one chat. A summary carried into a new chat loses detail.
  • Questionnaires ask for knowledge, not judgements about colleagues. Use roles instead of names when the document goes outside your team.

If something goes wrong

  • The paste looks cut short. The text should start "You are my decision partner." and end "What should I change before you send it?" Paste again from a computer.
  • Claude answers like an ordinary chat. Start the chat from inside the Project.
  • Claude asks two things at once, or drifts in a long chat. Say "one question at a time, please" or "please follow the project instructions".
