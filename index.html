Executive Decision Grill

Free Claude Project instructions for CEOs, CFOs, COOs, directors and managers, built to question a business decision, produce a decision map and draft a questionnaire for whoever holds the missing answers—with nothing to install.

Paste once into a Claude Project, then start a chat about your decision. Claude recommends. You decide.

Please read this first. Version 1.5 has been tested only in simulated conversations, not yet in the real Claude app, and it did not pass its final test: it sometimes changed a decision without asking or accepted a vague answer. Read every document Claude writes, and check it against what you said, before you use or send it. How it was tested.

Set it up in about five minutes

  1. Open claude.ai or the Claude desktop app. Choose Projects, click + New Project, and name it Executive Decision Grill.
  2. Copy the instructions with this button. It copies the whole text, about 23,000 characters.
    Open the text instead

  3. In your Project, choose Set project instructions (on some screens it is called Instructions), paste, and click Save instructions.
  4. Start a new chat inside the Project and say, for example, "Grill me on whether we should move customer service to an outside provider." If it worked, Claude restates your decision in one sentence and lists the smaller decisions inside it.

You need a Claude account. Anthropic's help pages say Projects are available on every plan, including Free (at most five Projects), and that you can set instructions for each Project. We have not tried it on a Free account, so check that the Set project instructions button appears. A computer is easier than a phone for the paste. Something not working? See If something goes wrong.

From a business decision to a decision map

Claude restates your decision in one sentence and lists the smaller decisions hiding inside it. Then it asks about them one at a time. Every decision question ends with My recommendation: and a reason, so "Agreed" is a complete reply. If your answer is vague, Claude asks again for a name, a number, a date or an example. You end with a decision map. Claude recommends. You decide.

1 · Decision Grill

"Grill me on whether we should drop our reseller in Central Europe."

You get: a decision map. Decided · What we said no to · Parked with someone else · Open · Not yet clear · Terms we agreed · Assumptions · Next step.

2 · Questionnaire

"Write a questionnaire for our head of IT about the warehouse go-live."

You get: a neutral questionnaire ready to paste into an email, with a sharing check for names next to criticism, unannounced plans and personal data.

Phrases that help

SayWhat happens
AgreedYou accept Claude's recommended answer and move on.
I don't knowThe item is parked with the person who would know, or left open. Claude is built not to make up an answer.
wait, what?Claude re-explains its last point in plain words, then asks again.
skip the questionsClaude asks the two most important questions, then writes the output.
let's wrap upStraight to the decision map, with the unfinished items listed.

Hard-to-undo choices stay yours. If you say "whatever you think" about a multi-year contract, Claude asks you to decide it in your own words.
